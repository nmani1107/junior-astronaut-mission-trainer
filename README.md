# Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer

**NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 — "Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer"**

Play it live (no login, no download, works on phone, tablet, and laptop):
**https://nmani1107.github.io/junior-astronaut-mission-trainer/**

A fun, interactive game for children **ages 7–12** that teaches how astronauts **train and prepare** for missions — living in space, staying healthy, working as a team, and making real decisions about life support, radiation shielding, power budgeting, food production, and communication delay. Players imagine themselves as astronauts on a Moon or Mars mission.

**One line:** Train like a real astronaut — 6 missions, 7 days, become a Commander.

## How to play

1. **Start with Voice Guide** (or **Play without voice**, or **▶ Play Now as Guest** — one tap straight into the missions, no name needed; a guest can add a name later from the profile card).
2. Type your first name — it stays on this device only, just for your training badge. No account, no login, nothing is sent anywhere.
3. **Choose your level:** Easy (ages 7–8) or Commander Hard (age 10+, faster timers, more hazards, less time). After you pick a level, the Level Science Behind This pop-up opens first, so the science is learned before the missions are chosen.
4. **Choose your training mission** — 6 missions. The first time you play a mission each day, the **Science Behind This** pop-up opens first (science only, audio plays by default) — you learn the science, then tap **Start Playing**. On Try Again and later plays that day it goes straight to the game. The 🔬 Science button is always there during play too.
5. Finish **7 days + the 4 main missions** to become a **Commander** and earn your certificate (with your name, the date, and "Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer — NASA Space Apps 2026" — screenshot it as proof). Moon Base Defender and Earth to Moon Journey are bonus missions.
6. **Switch Cadet / Add New Cadet** — each cadet keeps their own progress (day, XP, stars, badges, level) on the same device. Closing the tab never wipes progress.

## The 6 missions

| Mission | What you do | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| **Spacewalk** | Catch falling tools and supplies, dodge falling asteroids and rocks — collect 8 tools in 45 seconds (Easy) | Moving safely in space, looking all around, staying calm under pressure |
| **Exercise** | Tap the glowing pad as fast as you can — the astronaut on screen does the same motion (running, cycling, lifting) | Why astronauts exercise every day in space (microgravity bone and muscle loss) |
| **Moon Base Keeper** | Balance oxygen, food, power, and your radiation shield — keep the base alive for 40 seconds (Easy) | **The Big Trade-Off:** oxygen, power, and food compete for one power supply — every helper has an exact power cost, and saving power today means oxygen is low tomorrow. Your oxygen, power, and food are **shared across all 7 days** — what you save on one day helps the next day |
| **Space Weather Watch** | Watch a NASA motion on screen (launch, solar flare, CME, auroras, orbiting, docking, landing) and answer "What is this called?" — 7 questions, motions and answer order shuffled every game | Real space weather, solar storms, and how they affect astronauts |
| **Moon Base Defender** | Clear friendly Moon Dust and bring light to a dark base — start at Strength 2, answer NASA questions right to get stronger (+2 each) | Teamwork, light, and real NASA facts — no fighting, no scary content, kid-friendly only |
| **Earth to Moon Journey** | Steer your spaceship to the Moon (arrows, D-pad, or drag), dodge asteroids (a hit just puts you back in a safe spot — never game over), collect 3 parts and answer 3 questions to fix a problem mid-flight, meet Moon Friends, clean the dust, then teleport home in about 1 second — the flight starts inside the Earth, rises through the atmosphere with flames, Earth slowly shrinks as you fly away, the Moon grows as you approach, about 15 seconds Earth → problem, 16 seconds problem → Moon. If the Moon clock runs out, try again | **Communication delay:** Mission Control messages visibly travel and arrive after 1.3 seconds (and the ~22-minute Mars delay is explained). **Mars side:** pick a real Mars Trek landing site — Jezero, Gale, or Elysium — with real conditions, tied to the live Mars rover photo. Real pictures drawn by the kids are used in the flight: comet, Earth, Moon, Milky Way, SLS launch, and shuttle fire |

After every mission, win or lose, you get one line: **"Today I learned that…"** — the one real lesson from that mission, spoken and shown.

## Every day is different

Each training day has a **space-weather event** from live NASA DONKI data (or a curated rotation when offline): a calm day makes Spacewalk easier, a storm day drains the Habitat shield faster, a solar-flare day makes Weather Watch crackle. The day's event is shown on the home screen and inside the affected mission.

## NASA data — live and curated, labelled honestly

| Source | How it's used | Why we used it |
|---|---|---|
| **NASA Open APIs — LIVE** | APOD (Astronomy Picture of the Day) and Mars rover photos, shown in "Live from NASA" | Real pictures from real NASA missions, so kids see space as it really is |
| **DONKI space weather — LIVE** | Real solar flares and geomagnetic storms power the "Live Space Weather" panel, Weather Watch alerts, and the daily space-weather event | Real space weather decides real astronaut safety |
| **OSDR (NASA Open Science Data Repository) — curated** | Astronaut body-in-space facts in Exercise | Real research on what space does to the human body |
| **RadLab — curated** | Radiation facts in Moon Base Keeper and Weather Watch | Real radiation dosimetry, in plain words |
| **Mars Trek — curated** | Mars mission facts and the Jezero / Gale / Elysium landing-site choice | Real Mars maps and landing sites |
| **CSA space resources for STEM/youth — curated** | Training structure | How real astronaut training is organised for kids |
| **CSA food-production-on-the-Moon toolkit — curated** | Food system in Moon Base Keeper | How food could be grown on the Moon |

Curated facts are labelled **"Curated facts — not live"** — nothing static is ever presented as live. If the internet is off or NASA is unreachable, the game still plays fully on curated facts, with 📴 fallbacks — no blank screens.

Recorded by the family, with browser-voice fallback and **word-by-word highlighting** (the exact spoken word lights up and scrolls into view) on every screen.
- **Music:** a soft original music-box tune in C major, synthesized live, plays quietly only inside missions, ducks to a whisper whenever a voice speaks, and stops on the home screen or with Sound Off.
- **Science pop-ups:** every mission has a "Science Behind This" pop-up (science only, audio plays by default, 🔊 Listen replays, ⏹ Stop stops it). It opens automatically the first time you play a mission each day, so every kid learns the science before playing.
- **Question & answer audio:** every quiz reads itself aloud — the question and its choices play when the question appears, and the real NASA fact is read after answering — with a 🔊 Hear the Question / ⏹ Stop button on each question screen (Weather Watch, Defender, Journey gate, and Journey fix-it/clean-up questions). Word-by-word highlighting follows the reading.
- **No automatic navigation:** when any audio finishes, the game stays on the same screen and waits — the kid taps a button to move on. Nothing jumps ahead on a timer.
- **For Parents & Teachers:** a Dashboard tab with what kids learn, how to play together as a family or classroom, and 3 discussion questions for after playing.
- **Difficulty:** Easy (7–8) and Commander Hard (10+). **Support:** browsers from 2016–2026 (full support 2020+; graceful text-only fallback on older ones). Tested in Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit (the Safari engine) across phone, tablet, laptop, and desktop. No horizontal scrolling, big tap targets (48–56px), reduced-motion respected.

## Tech — technology used, devices, and browsers

One self-contained `index.html` (~2.4 MB) — all missions, voices, music, CSS, and JavaScript inlined. No framework, no build step, no backend, no server, no database. Progress is saved in browser localStorage on that device only — nothing is sent anywhere.

- **Technology:** plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript in a single file. Sounds and music are synthesized live with the Web Audio API (original music-box tune in C major, plus button taps, chimes, and rocket sounds). Spoken instructions and quiz readings use the browser's built-in speech (speechSynthesis), and the family's own recorded voices (MP3) play the welcome and mission introductions. Live NASA data is fetched straight from api.nasa.gov and NASA's DONKI API by the game code itself — no middle server.
- **Devices:** phones, tablets, laptops, and desktops — touch, mouse, keyboard arrows, and on-screen D-pad all work. Big tap targets (48–56px), no horizontal scrolling, reduced-motion respected.
- **Browsers:** Chrome, Firefox, and Safari (WebKit), plus Edge — browsers from 2016 onwards. Full support on 2020 and newer (Chrome 80+, Edge 80+, Safari 13+, Firefox 75+); 2016–2019 browsers play the full game with a graceful text-only fallback if voice or audio is missing. Tested end-to-end in Chromium, Firefox, and WebKit.
- **Offline:** the game plays fully with no internet, using curated real NASA facts (labelled "Curated facts — not live"), so a classroom or a plane is fine.
- **Full specs:** 6 missions, 7-day Cadet-to-Commander journey, 9 badges, 21 stars, 2 difficulty levels (Easy ages 7–8, Commander Hard age 10+), 7 family-recorded voices, daily space-weather event, shared oxygen/power/food across days, multiple cadet profiles on one device, guest play with no name needed.
